The Voice Is the Bill: Benchmarking an AI Paper Tutor on Sarvam

Prateek Mehra · guru · 6 min read ·

Published 27 Nov 2026

The question

Guru is a little app I built that explains research papers one paragraph at a time, out loud, in Kanpuriya Hindi, while it draws on a whiteboard. Every paragraph goes through three Sarvam AI models: sarvam-105b writes the lesson, Bulbul speaks it, and Saaras listens when you interrupt with a doubt.

It felt fast and cheap when I used it. But "felt" is not a number, so I measured it: how long each call takes, how many tokens it uses, and what it actually costs.

How I measured it

I picked five papers everyone knows: Attention Is All You Need, BERT, GPT-3, ResNet and DDPM. From each I took three paragraphs: the abstract, one method paragraph and one results paragraph. Each went through every endpoint 3 times. That's 225 calls, all made from my MacBook Air to Sarvam's API.

The script starts the real Guru server and talks to it over HTTP, like the browser does. It also listens in on the server's own calls to Sarvam, so token counts come straight from Sarvam's responses rather than my guesses. Costs use Sarvam's published prices from November 2026. The whole run took 10 minutes and cost ₹84.42, about $0.90.

Where the money went

I expected the 105B-parameter language model to be the expensive part. It was the cheapest. One explanation cost ₹0.026 on average, and answering a doubt cost ₹0.010. Speaking about 340 characters of that explanation cost ₹0.68 on bulbul:v4-flash and ₹1.02 on bulbul:v3.

Part of why the LLM is so cheap is prompt caching. Guru sends the whole paper as context on every call, which is 12,600 input tokens on average. But that prefix is the same every time, so about 89% of those tokens were billed at the cached rate, which is 40× cheaper. Without caching, an explanation would have cost about ₹0.135, still well under the voice.

Scaled up, reading all 61 paragraphs of the Transformer paper out loud would come to roughly ₹94. About ₹1.60 of that is the model thinking and the rest is the voice. That's arithmetic on the measured averages, not a separate run.

Is it fast enough?

An explanation takes about 3 seconds (p95 5.5 s). That sounds slow for a live conversation, but Guru never makes you wait for it: while you listen to one paragraph, which is about a minute of audio, the next one is already being prepared. Paragraph length barely mattered either. Short, medium and long paragraphs all came back in about 3.0 seconds, because the prompt asks for 4 to 8 spoken steps regardless of length.

The two voices were tied on speed (3.56 s vs 3.53 s median for about 340 characters, roughly 8× faster than playback). So v4-flash, which is 33% cheaper, is an easy default.

The one thing that broke

The LLM and the voices never failed: 180 calls, no errors, no retries, no rate limits. Speech-to-text failed a third of the time, and the reason was simple. Sarvam's real-time STT rejects audio longer than 30 seconds. I tested it by feeding Guru's own narration back in, and the longer clips crossed that line.

In the app, a student asking a doubt rarely talks for 30 seconds, but nothing stops them. I also found that the server retries any 400 error with a different audio type, so a too-long clip makes two doomed calls instead of one. Both are easy fixes: cap the recording at about 28 seconds, and only retry when the error is actually about the format.

What I'd tell you before you trust these numbers

  • This is one run of 225 calls from one laptop on a home connection. Latency includes my network, and a different time of day could move it.
  • BERT's explanations were oddly slow: 5 of 9 took 5–6.6 s with the same token counts as the fast ones. I can't tell from one run whether that was Sarvam or chance.
  • TTS was tested on the first ~340 characters of each narration, not the full ~760, and STT on Guru's own synthetic voice, not real students.
  • sarvam-105b is on introductory pricing until the end of 2026, so the LLM cost could change. The voice prices are the ones to watch anyway.
  • Nothing streams, so I couldn't measure time-to-first-token.

The conclusion

For an app that talks, the language model is not where the cost is. With caching, the 105B model that writes every lesson cost less than two rupees for the whole benchmark. The voice that reads it out cost nearly fifty times more. If I want Guru cheaper, the lever is fewer spoken characters or reusing audio, not a smaller LLM.

The benchmark script, raw results for all 225 calls and the full write-up are in the repo: github.com/prateek-mehra/guru.
